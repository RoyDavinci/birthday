Corrections, most important first
The answer is “A” about 72% of the time. Options are never shuffled, and in 26 of 36 choice questions the correct one is first. Tapping A all the way through scores very well. Shuffle per attempt and save the order in the session so a resume looks the same. The correct option is also the longest one in 15 of 36 questions, which is another tell.
A timeout throws away what the player picked. If they select an option or type something but the clock hits 0 before they press Submit, they get zero. Auto-submit whatever is in the box instead.
There is no Enter-to-submit. Text answers aren’t in a <form> and there is no key handling. On mobile this costs people seconds.
Number scoring is too forgiving.
Anything within 10% counts as fully correct. A guess of 6.5 for “6 languages” gets full marks and the “Correct!” message.
For small counts a percentage band doesn’t work. Guessing 3 or 5 siblings (answer 4) gets 75%, and 0 gets 25%. Make small integers exact, or allow ±1 for partial credit.
Q33 prints the range (₦50m to ₦100m) in the prompt, and any value in it scores 20 points. Don’t reveal the range.
type="number" inputs block commas and ₦, so the symbol-stripping in scoring.ts never runs. Eight-digit amounts are also hard to type and check. Use a text input with inputMode="numeric", live formatting, and ideally shorthand like “35m”.
Text matching is too strict.
It needs an exact string after normalising, and multi-part answers rely on hand-written orderings. “Nutri milk”, “tea & Chinese rice”, “react.js and node.js” and “hustle” all fail.
Use required keyword groups per answer (each with synonyms), so order doesn’t matter. Add a small typo tolerance, and give partial credit per part.
Double-check Q5. The prompt says “E no get where it ___” but the accepted answers start with “e”, which suggests the phrase may be slightly off.
Cross-question giveaways. Q17 (“which is NOT a favourite”) can be solved by elimination from Q13 and Q16. Q35’s answer (Japa) was just given away by Q34. Q2’s answer is hinted by Q1’s options. The “…only” wording in some distractors also telegraphs the right answer, as does the hedged “potentially a tech company” option. Joke distractors like “Buy everybody a car” are fun, but they make it easy.
The “Unanswered questions” stat on the results screen is always 0. Timeouts are recorded as answers. Count timedOut instead.
The timer isn’t clock-based. It chains setTimeout calls, which browsers throttle in background tabs. Store a deadline timestamp and compute the remaining time from it. This also removes the eslint-disable set-state-in-effect at the top of App.tsx.
Sharing and the results card.
The share text has no link, so it can’t pull anyone in.
html-to-image is flaky on iOS Safari, where it often needs two calls, and link.click() downloads don’t behave well there. Use navigator.share({ files }) where it’s supported.
Replace the alert() calls with a toast.
Answers and scores live in the browser. All answers are in the JS bundle, and the saved score can be edited in localStorage. That’s fine for a casual game, but not if there’s a leaderboard or a prize (see below).
